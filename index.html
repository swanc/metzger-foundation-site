Gustav Metzger

1926 — 2017

Gustav Metzger was a visionary artist and radical thinker. At the heart of his practice, which spanned over 65 years, are a series of constantly opposing yet interdependent forces — destruction and creation, the material and the immaterial, action and stillness.

The Gustav Metzger Foundation is a UK-based charity founded upon Metzger's death in 2017. During his lifetime, Metzger defined the organisation's mission by envisioning not only exhibitions of his work and furtherance of the political and philosophical ideas he espoused, but also through support for individuals working in the fields of the arts and environmental studies, and for initiatives “to combat the risk of global extinction arising from the activities of humans.”

Acid Action Painting, South Bank, London, 1961

Gustav Metzger, Recreation of First Public Demonstration of Auto-Destructive Art, South Bank, London, 1961. © The Gustav Metzger Foundation

From 1959, Metzger developed the concept of auto-destructive art based on the political and ecological themes of his era, such as the nuclear arms race and ecological destruction. In his first manifesto, he defined this as “a form of public art for industrial societies” that focuses on the twentieth century's potential for annihilation by means of self-destructive elements.

Auto-destructive art is primarily a form of public art for industrial societies. — Gustav Metzger, Auto-Destructive Art Manifesto, 1959

Together with John Sharkey, he initiated the Destruction in Art Symposium in London in 1966. This was followed in 1974 by a call for artists to support a three-year Art Strike (1977–1980), and he did, in fact, withdraw from the art world during those years.
